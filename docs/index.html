Core principle

Three primitives. Every professional service.

Professional trust should be cryptographically verifiable, AI-augmented and community-governed.

Identity-native trust

KERI AIDs and ACDC credentials replace institutional accreditation with cryptographic proof of qualification, licensing and professional standing.

  • ACDC credentials
  • Licensing
  • Delegation

Agentic automation

AI agents run professional workflows such as document review, compliance checks and risk assessment, with human oversight at decision boundaries.

  • Compliance checks

Programmable governance

Professional standards evolve through community consensus rather than centralised regulatory capture. Rules become code and compliance becomes continuous.

  • Standards by consensus
  • Rules as code

The Human Conductor model

Professionals conduct. Agents execute.

The professional is not replaced by AI. They become the conductor of an orchestra of autonomous agents.

Conductor

What stays human.

People provide strategic direction, ethical judgment and accountability. Agents handle execution, analysis and routine decisions.

  • Direction

    Define objectives, priorities and constraints for agent fleets.

  • Judgment

    Decide at boundaries that exceed agent authority or call for ethical reasoning.

  • Governance

    Set and evolve the rules under which agents operate.

  • Accountability

    Hold ultimate responsibility for outcomes, backed by their own verifiable professional credentials.

Use cases

Work that crosses domains.

Verified identity lets agents carry a matter across disciplines, with a conductor at every boundary.

  • Legal → Insurance

    Cross-domain claims resolution

    A claimant's KERI AID anchors their identity across insurer, legal counsel and medical provider. Agents gather evidence, assess liability and coordinate settlement, with every credential verified in real time.

    Conductor: steps in at liability disputes and settlement thresholds.

  • Compliance → Audit

    Continuous regulatory compliance

    Instead of annual audits, agents monitor regulatory change, assess impact and generate reports. Auditor findings are issued as verifiable credentials, building an immutable history.

    Conductor: the compliance officer reviews flagged items and approves remediation.

  • HR → Advisory

    Credential-verified expert matching

    Organisations find qualified professionals through ACDC credential matching. Agents scope engagements, verify expertise claims and coordinate deliverables.

    Conductor: the engagement manager makes the final selection from verified profiles.

  • Property → Finance

    Autonomous settlement coordination

    Agents coordinate buyer, seller, lender and title authority. Authorisations are verified via KERI delegation, and settlement conditions run as programmable rules with on-chain finality.

    Conductor: the conveyancer approves milestones and resolves exceptions.

Benefits & challenges

What it delivers. What still needs work.

Addressing the challenges takes gradual adoption, transparent governance design and active engagement with existing regulatory frameworks.

Benefits

Trust you can check.

Credentials, actions and outcomes become verifiable rather than asserted.

  • Verifiability

    Every professional credential, action and outcome is cryptographically verifiable rather than institutionally asserted.

  • Efficiency

    Agents run routine workflows at machine speed, freeing professionals for judgment and strategy.

  • Portability

    Credentials and client records move with their holders, not locked within institutional silos.

  • Transparency

    Fee structures, governance rules and compliance states are programmable and auditable.

  • Resilience

    Decentralised infrastructure and community governance reduce single points of failure in professional service delivery.

Challenges

Open questions.

The work still ahead before this becomes the default.

  • Regulatory recognition

    Existing frameworks may not yet recognise cryptographic credentials as equivalent to institutional accreditation.

  • Governance complexity

    Community-governed standards need robust decision-making to maintain quality and accountability.

  • Identity infrastructure adoption

    Widespread adoption of KERI/ACDC infrastructure is still in its early stages.

  • Professional liability

    The legal framework for liability in human-conductor and AI-agent relationships is still evolving.

  • Interoperability

    Credential schemas and agent protocols must be standardised across jurisdictions and professional domains.
